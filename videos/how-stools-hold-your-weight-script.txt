How Can a Stool Hold Your Weight? — narration script

[How Can a Stool Hold Your Weight?]
Hi friends! Have you ever wondered how a little stool can hold you up? Let's find out!

[Gravity Pulls Down]
The Earth is always pulling you down. That pull is called gravity. When you sit, your weight pushes down on the seat.

[The Seat Shares the Push]
The seat spreads your push out into all the legs. Each leg carries a share of the load, so no one leg has to do it all.

[The Floor Pushes Back]
The legs push down on the floor. And the floor pushes back up, just as hard! Push down, push up. They balance, so nothing moves.

[Three or Four Legs]
Why three or four legs? Spread out feet make a wide base. Your balance point, right in the middle of you, stays inside the feet. So the stool stays steady.

[Lean Too Far... Tip!]
But if you lean too far, your balance point goes outside the feet. Then, whoops! The stool tips over.

[Safety Tip]
So sit in the middle, keep your feet down, and don't rock or lean back on a stool.

[Strong and Straight]
Stools are made of strong stuff, like wood or metal. Straight legs are great at being squished from the top. That is called compression. A straight leg can hold a lot!

[Cross Braces]
These bars between the legs are called cross braces. They stop the legs from spreading out sideways, so the stool stays strong.

[Try It at Home!]
Try this! Stand four paper cups upside down, and gently put a book on top. The cups share the weight, and hold it up! Ask a grown-up to help.

[Now You Know!]
Gravity pulls down, the legs share the push, and the floor pushes back up. That's how a stool holds you up!
