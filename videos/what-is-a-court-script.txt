What Is a Court? — narration script

[What Is a Court?]
Hi there! Have you ever had an argument about what's fair, like who gets the last cookie? Grown-ups have disagreements too. And sometimes they need help sorting them out. That's what a court is for!

[A place for fairness]
A court is a special place where people go to solve big problems in a fair way, by following rules that everyone shares. These rules are called laws.

[The Judge]
In charge of the courtroom is the judge. The judge is like a referee in a game. They make sure everyone follows the rules, and that nobody gets treated unfairly.

[The Lawyers]
Each side gets a lawyer. A lawyer is a helper who knows lots about the rules. They speak up for their side and explain their story as clearly as they can.

[The Witnesses]
Witnesses are people who saw or know something important. They promise to tell the truth, then they answer questions, so everyone can learn what really happened.

[The Jury]
Sometimes there is a jury. A jury is a group of ordinary people from the town. They listen carefully to everything, then work together to decide what they think is true.

[Fair for everyone]
The most important idea in court is fairness. Both sides get a turn to talk. Everyone must listen. And the decision should be based on facts, not on who is richest, loudest, or most popular.

[Why have courts?]
So why do we have courts? Because without them, people might try to solve problems by shouting or fighting. Courts help us settle problems peacefully, with rules that are the same for everybody.

[Fair rules, calm talking]
So next time you take turns, listen to both sides, and tell the truth, you're doing exactly what a court does. Great job, fairness helper!
